# Collection validation

Validated on 2026-09-05. Re-validated on 2026-09-06 after: an added illustrative "TOYOTA" badge on the Supra's front fascia (the source model had none); the Williams FW14B, first rebalanced from a solid-blue synthetic livery to a white/blue/yellow synthetic split, then replaced outright with a different, properly textured source model carrying real Canon/Camel/Elf/Labatt's-era sponsor decals; the Aston Martin DB5 recolored from the source model's teal-green to Silver Birch; twenty-six new road cars — Rolls-Royce Cullinan, Kia Forte, Audi R8, Ferrari LaFerrari, Ferrari 250 GTO, Lamborghini Urus, Porsche 959, Porsche 918 Spyder, Ford Model T, Bugatti Type 57SC Atlantic, Volkswagen Beetle, Citroën 2CV, BMW M4, BMW i8, Mercedes-AMG GT, Mercedes-Benz S-Class, Ford GT40, Shelby Cobra 427, Chevrolet Corvette Stingray (C2), Lamborghini Miura, Ferrari Testarossa, Toyota 2000GT, Datsun 240Z, Lancia Delta HF Integrale, Honda NSX, and Austin Mini Cooper S; and ten new Formula 1 legends — Lotus 49, McLaren MP4/4, Ferrari 312T, Williams FW07, Tyrrell P34, Brabham BT52, Lotus 72, Ferrari F2004, Red Bull RB6, and Ferrari F310. Each new source was checked for provenance red flags before use; candidates were rejected after their mesh names or listing text disclosed non-CC origins (a paid-asset watermark, or mobile-game/racing-sim/file-share sourcing contradicting the license tag) for the Cullinan, several Ferraris, the BMW i8/M4, the Ford GT40, the Honda NSX-R, and a Tyrrell P34 candidate whose material list named a CSR2 mobile-game asset. Two conversions (Chevrolet Corvette C2, Datsun 240Z) needed a length-axis orientation fix identical to the earlier Bugatti Atlantic issue; two others (Austin Mini Cooper S, and the Mercedes-AMG GT/Chevrolet Corvette C2 pair) had stray studio watermarks or presentation floors excluded during conversion. Two conversion pipeline bugs were found and fixed while adding the F1 legends: the Brabham BT52's single joined mesh had every one of its 51 materials attached to every separated piece, misclassifying the entire car as wheels, fixed by stripping unused material slots right after the loose-geometry split; and the Lotus 72 and Ferrari F2004 sources imported with every material alpha-blended (a packed specular channel misread as an alpha mask), fixed by forcing those two cars back to opaque. All were verified with `validate-collection.mjs`, `validate-explosion.mjs`, and `validate-touch.mjs`, and card renders were generated or regenerated with `render-car-cards.py`.

A texture-quality pass followed: every original source download still cached from this session's work was checked for its native texture resolution against what each model actually had embedded. Eight models — Williams FW07, Tyrrell P34, Lamborghini Urus, Mercedes S-Class, Shelby Cobra, Rolls-Royce Cullinan, Kia Forte, and McLaren F1 GTR Longtail — had native source textures above their embedded resolution and were re-converted at up to 4096px (up from 2048px, or 1024px for the McLaren, which predated an earlier resolution increase). Re-conversion reused each model's known real-world length and orientation flag, and every result was re-verified: bounding-box dimensions matched the prior committed model exactly, piece counts matched exactly, and card renders were regenerated and visually checked. One reconversion mistake was caught and corrected: Porsche 930 was briefly run through the wrong conversion script (`convert-collection-model.py` instead of its actual `convert-classic-cars.py` pipeline), producing 147 pieces instead of 318; this was caught immediately by the piece-count mismatch, reverted from git, and not repeated. Four more sources — Citroën 2CV, BMW M3 E30, Porsche 930, and Porsche 959 — also have native 4096px textures, but re-converting them at that resolution multiplied their file size 2-3x (Citroën 2CV alone would have grown from 74MB to 220MB) for no meaningful visual gain in this viewer, so those four were reverted to their prior 2048px-capped versions. No geometry was changed by this pass; the pipeline has never decimated imported meshes, so mesh detail was already at full source fidelity going in. The remaining ~48 models' own source textures were already below the old 2048px cap (many natively 1024px or smaller), so the cap was never the limiting factor for them and they were left untouched. TypeScript, lint, both production builds, `validate-collection.mjs` (all sixty-seven models, matching piece counts), `validate-explosion.mjs`, and `validate-touch.mjs` all pass after this pass.

A third pass fixed several rendering bugs and changed the collection roster. Fixed: the Lamborghini Countach's card orientation (was showing the rear, now shows the front, matching every other card) and its paint color (white to electric blue); the Ford GT40 floating visibly above its display plinth (two leftover default-primitive spheres in the source, excluded from the pipeline, had been used to compute the floor offset before being silently dropped from the final export); the Ford Model T rendering off-center on its turntable (a giant "Text" watermark prop in the source, the same class of bug as the Volkswagen Beetle's "KDF 38" prop, skewed the centering); and the car-collection card's brand-name text and color dot being unreadable for black-painted cars (both previously used the raw paint color as text/fill color; both now blend toward a light color so they stay legible). Replaced: the Mercedes-AMG GT (its old source had corrupted, wildly out-of-scale geometry in the raw mesh itself, not just a stray prop, so patching wasn't possible), the Toyota Supra Mk4 (its "illustrative TOYOTA badge" was actually mounted on the rear fascia, invisible from the fixed 3/4-front card angle; the replacement shows a badge from the front instead), and the Lamborghini Miura (its old source was a single flat-colored material with no real differentiation between body, glass, and trim; the replacement is properly multi-material, after excluding a large marble-textured ground plane and recoloring one placeholder pink material). Removed: the Tyrrell P34, at the user's request; a specifically-requested replacement (a 1974 Tyrrell 007) was not downloadable (Editorial license), so the car was not replaced. Added: Ford Mustang Shelby GT500 (Ddiaz Design), BMW M3 GTR (Dave Love SketchFab), McLaren F1 1993 road car (Alex.Ka.), and four 2025-season Formula 1 cars — McLaren MCL39, Ferrari SF-25, Red Bull RB21, and (in place of a nonexistent downloadable 2025 Mercedes model) the 2024 Mercedes W15, all by Abu Saif. Two of these new sources (McLaren F1 1993, and separately a first attempt at the Mustang GT500 "Eleanor" variant) needed the same stray-geometry exclusion pattern as the GT40/Model T fixes above; the Eleanor Mustang conversion additionally hung indefinitely on an unusually large source file and was abandoned in favor of a different, smaller GT500 source. Several other user-requested links were checked and rejected as not usable: a Kia Forte GT 2019 and a Mercedes-AMG G63 (both Editorial-licensed, not downloadable), and five further F1 additions (2021 Alpine A521, 2023 Alfa Romeo C43, BMW Sauber F1.07, Williams FW38-B, and a Hummer EV) that had no reusable license or were not downloadable. All 66 models pass `validate-collection.mjs`, `validate-explosion.mjs`, `validate-touch.mjs`, TypeScript, lint, and both production builds; local HTTP checks confirm all car routes return 200 and the removed Tyrrell P34 route returns 404.

A fourth pass fixed two more card orientations and added one car. The Ferrari F2004 and Lotus 72 cards were both found facing backward (rear toward the camera) compared to every other card; both were re-converted with `flip` instead of `native`, with no change to geometry, materials, or piece counts. Added: Aston Martin One-77 (Ddiaz Design, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Its source had the same leftover unmaterialed "Icosphere" reference object already seen in the Ford GT40 source, which had been silently skewing the scale calculation (the exported car came out at 5cm long before this was caught); the fix was generalized from a Ford GT40-specific exclusion to drop any such object regardless of car. Five more requested links were checked and rejected: a replacement Aston Martin DB5, an F1 Mercedes W11 2020, an F1 2025 Williams FW47, an F1 2025 Alpine A525, and two further Kia Forte candidates — all either unlicensed, Editorial-licensed, or (confirmed by re-checking the Sketchfab API directly, twice) simply not downloadable, with no download control present on the page itself. All 67 models pass `validate-collection.mjs`, `validate-explosion.mjs`, `validate-touch.mjs`, TypeScript, lint, and both production builds.

A fifth pass added twenty-seven road cars from a batch of forty-nine requested links (Porsche Boxster Spyder, Ferrari 12Cilindri, Ferrari F50, Ferrari F8 Tributo, Ferrari F8 Spider, Ferrari Portofino, Rolls-Royce Ghost, Rolls-Royce Wraith, Mercedes-Maybach S-Class, Mercedes-Benz 190, Mercedes-Benz V8 Biturbo, Mercedes-AMG SL 63, BMW M4 CSL, BMW 5 Series (E34), BMW M3 (E46), Nissan GT-R (R35), Nissan Versa, Range Rover Evoque, Land Rover Defender 110 Double Cab Pickup, Jaguar XJ220, Jaguar XJ Sport (X308), Bentley Mulliner Batur, Bentley Continental GT, Cadillac Escalade ESV, Toyota GR86, Toyota Land Cruiser 250, and Pagani Huayra) and replaced the Kia Forte a second time. Sixteen requested links were rejected as not downloadable or Editorial/unlicensed, two were exact Sketchfab-UID duplicates of already-owned sources (Mercedes 300 SL, BMW M3 E30), and three more were real-world duplicates of cars already in the collection under different sources (Porsche 959, Ferrari LaFerrari, Ferrari 250 GTO) and were skipped; a requested Mercedes 190/W201 pair resolved to the same real car, so only one source was kept. Two sources (Mercedes-Maybach, BMW M4 CSL) used their raw file's X axis as vehicle length rather than the pipeline's default Y and needed the `x-length` orientation flag — `native` had inflated their reported width past 11 meters. The Mercedes-Maybach source additionally had a stray default-primitive reference cube (exact ±1-unit vertices) below the car skewing its floor calculation, excluded by name. The Kia Forte's prior AI-reconstructed source (foggy glass, a garbled dashboard sticker, no separate glass/cabin geometry) was replaced with a hand-modeled 2010 Kia Forte Koup (Ddiaz Design, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). The car-collection view also gained a search box and alphabetical (make + model) sorting for both the road-car and Formula 1 collections, replacing the previous fixed catalog order. All 94 models pass `validate-collection.mjs`, `validate-explosion.mjs`, `validate-touch.mjs`, TypeScript, lint, and both production builds; local HTTP checks confirm all car routes return 200.

A sixth pass replaced the Ferrari Roma (Ddiaz Design, CC BY 4.0) and added a Lamborghini Aventador SVJ, a Tesla Roadster, and fifteen Formula 1 cars spanning 2019–2023 (Red Bull RB19, Ferrari SF90, Mercedes W10, Alfa Romeo C41, Williams FW42, Alfa Romeo C38, Renault RS19, Racing Point RP20, AlphaTauri AT01, Ferrari SF21, Mercedes W11, Williams FW43, McLaren MP4/19B, Alpine A521, and Renault RS20). The collection search input's native browser clear icon was also removed (changed from `type="search"` to `type="text"`), leaving only the custom clear button. Two sources were downloaded and converted but ultimately dropped after repeated fix attempts failed: a Lamborghini SVJ Carbonado Twin Turbo Mansory, whose flat floor/backdrop exclusion only shifted its ~5-meter width bug to the next-widest stray geometry rather than fixing it (a clean stock-styled Aventador SVJ is used instead); and a McLaren MP4/27, whose ground-shadow plane excluded cleanly but whose wing and bodywork pieces turned out to be mirrored/duplicated several meters off-axis, a systemic geometry fault rather than one stray prop. A Dodge Challenger hung indefinitely on two separate conversion attempts (25+ minutes of active but unproductive CPU time each) and was not added, the same failure mode as the earlier Mustang "Eleanor" source. Of a batch of forty-nine road-car links and twenty-two F1 links, sixteen and five were respectively rejected for licensing (Sketchfab "Free Standard" or "Standard" licenses, Editorial licenses, or not downloadable at all, including one Tesla Cybertruck rejected despite carrying a CC BY-NC-SA tag). The Citroën 2CV was re-examined after a user report; its non-glass materials were found alpha-blended for no reason (the same latent-transparency bug fixed earlier for the Lotus 72/Ferrari F2004) and are now forced opaque, though visual inspection confirmed the reported "odd roof" was in fact the real Citroën 2CV Charleston's correctly modeled folding-canvas sunroof, not a defect. All 111 models pass `validate-collection.mjs`, `validate-explosion.mjs`, `validate-touch.mjs`, TypeScript, lint, and both production builds; local HTTP checks confirm all car routes return 200.
