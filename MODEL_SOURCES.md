## Adaptations

Orientation and scale were normalized, presentation floors removed, physical materials adapted, and disconnected source geometry separated or clustered for browser interaction. Embedded textures were resized where needed. Card previews are renders of the exported meshes. The source detail level varies between models.

The F40 has red paint and the Porsche 930 has yellow paint. The Porsche radio antenna was removed. The Phantom uses midnight-blue paint, and the Roma uses red paint. The Diablo retains its low-poly yellow SV appearance. The BMW M1 retains its Procar livery, and the M3 uses red paint. The DB5 uses Silver Birch paint, the shade driven in the Bond films, replacing the source model's teal-green. Formula 1 liveries are from the attributed source models.

The Range Rover and McLaren F1 GTR Longtail derivatives and their rendered previews retain the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license: attribution, noncommercial use, and share-alike terms apply. The Lamborghini Urus derivative retains the CC BY-NC 4.0 license: attribution and noncommercial use apply, without a share-alike requirement. BlendKit models remain subject to the linked asset license.

The Tesla is a pre-refresh source model with an unverified exact year. Its battery, drive units, and suspension are separately identified illustrative additions. The Supra's source model had no badge geometry; a small illustrative "TOYOTA" badge was added to the front fascia to match the real car's appearance. The Williams FW14B uses its source model's embedded livery textures (Canon/Camel/Elf/Labatt's-era sponsor decals) directly; an earlier conversion of a different, texture-less source asset had reconstructed a synthetic paint split instead, since replaced. The Cullinan's source is a low-poly study, comparable in detail to the Diablo; a separate, more detailed Cullinan source model was rejected after its embedded mesh names showed it was redistributed without proper rights from a paid-asset source. The Audi R8 and Lamborghini Urus sources are aftermarket widebody builds rather than stock configurations. The Ferrari 250 GTO, Porsche 918 Spyder, and Citroën 2CV source textures have specular highlights baked directly into the paint, which can read as light blotches under the studio's own lighting; this reflects the low-budget source assets rather than a conversion defect. The Volkswagen Beetle's source model credits a base mesh by a second Sketchfab creator, reworked by its uploader; a stray decorative "KDF 38" text prop from the source scene was excluded during conversion. The BMW M4 and Mercedes S-Class sources are stock cars; the Mercedes S-Class is a Brabus 850 widebody variant of the W223 chassis. Multiple higher-profile BMW i8 and M4 candidates were rejected for the same reason as the earlier Ferraris: their listings disclosed non-CC origins (an Assetto Corsa racing-sim credit) despite the CC BY tag. Several higher-detail candidates for the Ferrari 250 GTO and F8 Tributo were rejected: one Sketchfab account's listings for these carried disclosures that the underlying meshes were scraped from mobile racing games (Real Racing 3, CSR2) or third-party file shares with commercial use "strictly prohibited," contradicting the CC BY tag the account applied to them. The Ford GT40 and Datsun 240Z sources are heavily modified restomod/rally-mod builds rather than stock cars, in the same vein as the Audi R8 and Urus; a higher-profile GT40 candidate was rejected after its listing disclosed "Racing Master" mobile-game sourcing despite its CC BY tag, and the same disclosure ruled out an otherwise well-reviewed Honda NSX-R candidate. The Ford GT40 asset is CC BY-NC-SA. The Lamborghini Miura's source model is untextured and uses a single shared material for the whole car (including glass and tires), overridden to a uniform orange. The Bugatti Atlantic, Chevrolet Corvette C2, and Datsun 240Z sources use a different length-axis convention than the rest of the pipeline and needed the `x-length` orientation flag; converting any of them with the usual `native` setting stretches the wrong axis, since the scale step normalizes whichever axis the flag designates as length. The Austin Mini Cooper S and Volkswagen Beetle sources each carried a stray studio watermark (a floating logo/text prop unrelated to the vehicle), excluded during conversion the same way as the Mercedes-AMG GT and Chevrolet Corvette C2 presentation floors. Ten Formula 1 legends were added: Lotus 49, McLaren MP4/4, Ferrari 312T, Williams FW07, Tyrrell P34, Brabham BT52, Lotus 72, Ferrari F2004, Red Bull RB6, and Ferrari F310. A Tyrrell P34 candidate from another account was rejected after one of its materials was named `2021 Lamborghini Essenza SCV12_CSR2_Coloured`, disclosing that it was assembled from assets ripped from the CSR2 mobile game despite its CC BY tag; the vecarz source used instead is untextured in its "Original format" download but the same listing's separately converted glTF download carries correctly linked textures, which was used instead. The Brabham BT52 source is a single joined mesh with all 51 of its materials assigned to every resulting piece once separated by loose geometry, which had made every piece match the wheel/tire/brake keyword filter; the conversion script now strips unused material slots from each separated piece right after the loose-geometry split, fixing this for any future single-mesh source. The Lotus 72 and Ferrari F2004 sources import with every material flagged alpha-blended (a packed specular texture's channel was read as an alpha mask), which risks depth-sorting holes across separated pieces in WebGL even though it renders solid in Blender; both are forced back to fully opaque during conversion. A texture-quality pass re-ran conversion for every model whose original downloaded source was still available and whose source textures actually exceeded the pipeline's previous 2048px cap: Williams FW07, Tyrrell P34, Lamborghini Urus, Mercedes S-Class, Shelby Cobra, Rolls-Royce Cullinan, Kia Forte, and McLaren F1 GTR Longtail now embed textures up to 4096px (the McLaren F1 GTR had been converted before the cap was ever raised past 1024px, so this is a 4x jump for it). Four other sources with native 4096px textures — Citroën 2CV, BMW M3 E30, Porsche 930, and Porsche 959 — were also re-run at 4096px but reverted: each has enough separate, mostly-opaque 4K PNG textures that the higher cap multiplied their file size by 2-3x (one nearly tripled) for a gain not worth that cost in an interactive multi-model viewer, so they stay at the prior 2048px cap. The remaining models' own source textures never exceeded 2048px (many are natively 1024px, 512px, or lower), so raising the cap further would not change their embedded resolution at all; re-running them was not attempted. Geometry was not altered by this pass — the pipeline has never decimated or simplified imported meshes, so every model already carries its source's full mesh detail. The Mercedes-AMG GT's original source (Yan Carvalho) was replaced outright: its main body mesh itself had corrupted, wildly out-of-scale geometry (parts of the mesh spanned 10+ meters in world space even in the raw source, not from a stray prop), which was not fixable by excluding a few objects. The replacement source (jseopd9) uses its raw file's X axis as vehicle length rather than the pipeline's default Y, needing the `x-reverse` orientation flag to both remap that axis and face the car forward for the card. Card renders for every model were also changed to always scale each car to the same reference length in frame, rather than only shrinking cars longer than that reference; previously only cars close to that length (the Tesla, coincidentally) filled the card the way the interactive viewer's auto-fit camera always does, while shorter cars rendered visibly smaller and the corrupted AMG GT rendered stretched past the frame edges. A second pass fixed several longstanding rendering bugs and made further additions and replacements. The Lamborghini Countach's paint was changed from white to electric blue, and its card orientation was corrected from a rear to a front 3/4 view (`flip` instead of `native`), matching the rest of the collection. The Ford GT40 source included two leftover default-primitive spheres positioned well below the car, which the export catalog silently dropped but which had already been used to compute the model's floor offset, leaving the real geometry floating above the ground; excluding them by name fixed this. The Ford Model T source included a giant "Text" watermark prop (the same class of bug as the Volkswagen Beetle's "KDF 38" prop), whose size and off-center position skewed the model's centering, making the car render off to one side of the turntable; excluding it centers the car correctly. The Toyota Supra Mk4's original source was replaced (Lexyc16, CC BY-NC 4.0): its illustrative "TOYOTA" badge (mentioned in earlier notes as being on the front fascia) turned out to actually be mounted on the rear fascia, invisible from the fixed 3/4-front card camera; the new source shows a visible grille badge from the front instead, and the old source is no longer used. The Lamborghini Miura's original untextured single-material source was replaced (elenstesh, CC BY 4.0) with a properly multi-material one; the replacement's own source scene included a large flat marble-textured ground plane that skewed the whole model's scale, fixed by excluding it, and one placeholder pink headlight-bezel material was recolored to a neutral dark trim. The McLaren F1 (1993, Alex.Ka., CC BY-NC 4.0) source likewise included a floor plane and a second flat reference plane, both excluded the same way. The car-collection card's brand-name text and color-swatch dot used the paint color directly, which was unreadable for black-painted cars against the dark card background; both now blend toward a light color so they stay legible regardless of paint darkness. The Tyrrell P34 was removed from the collection at the user's request; a specifically-requested replacement source (Tyrrell 007, 1974) turned out not to be downloadable (Editorial license), so no replacement was made. A requested Kia Forte GT 2019 replacement and a requested Mercedes-AMG G63 addition were also not downloadable (Editorial license); several other requested F1 additions (2021 Alpine A521, 2023 Alfa Romeo C43, BMW Sauber F1.07, Williams FW38-B, Hummer EV) had no license or were Editorial-only and were not added. Four other requested additions were added: Ford Mustang Shelby GT500 (Ddiaz Design, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, a different, more stock-styled GT500 than the "Eleanor" movie-car variant that was tried first — that conversion hung indefinitely on an unusually large source file and was abandoned), BMW M3 GTR (Dave Love SketchFab, the Need for Speed Most Wanted Le Mans livery), and four 2025-season Formula 1 cars: McLaren MCL39, Ferrari SF-25, and Red Bull RB21 (all Abu Saif, CC BY 4.0), plus a Mercedes entry using the W15 instead of a genuine W16, since no downloadable 2025 Mercedes F1 car model existed at the time of adding. The Ferrari F2004 and Lotus 72 cards were both found rendering the car's rear toward the camera instead of the front, unlike every other card; both were re-converted with the `flip` orientation flag instead of `native` to face them forward, with no other change to geometry, materials, or classification. Added: Aston Martin One-77 (Ddiaz Design, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Its source had the same leftover default-primitive "Icosphere" object (no material, positioned far from the car) already seen in the Ford GT40 source; rather than add another car-specific exclusion, the fix was generalized to drop any unmaterialed "Icosphere" object regardless of car, since it is never legitimate vehicle geometry. Several other requested links in this round were checked and rejected as not usable: a replacement Aston Martin DB5 (no license set, not downloadable), an F1 Mercedes W11 2020 (Editorial), an F1 2025 Williams FW47 (no license set, not downloadable), an F1 2025 Alpine A525 (no license set, not downloadable), and two further Kia Forte candidates (both Editorial-licensed, not downloadable — confirmed by both the Sketchfab API and the absence of any download control on the page itself). All mesh labels and groupings are descriptive interpretations rather than OEM part identifiers.

A fifth pass added twenty-seven road cars from a batch of forty-nine requested links: Porsche Boxster Spyder, Ferrari 12Cilindri, Ferrari F50, Ferrari F8 Tributo, Ferrari F8 Spider, Ferrari Portofino, Rolls-Royce Ghost, Rolls-Royce Wraith, Mercedes-Maybach S-Class, Mercedes-Benz 190, Mercedes-Benz V8 Biturbo, Mercedes-AMG SL 63, BMW M4 CSL, BMW 5 Series (E34), BMW M3 (E46), Nissan GT-R (R35), Nissan Versa, Range Rover Evoque, Land Rover Defender 110 (Double Cab Pickup), Jaguar XJ220, Jaguar XJ Sport (X308), Bentley Mulliner Batur, Bentley Continental GT, Cadillac Escalade ESV, Toyota GR86, Toyota Land Cruiser 250, and Pagani Huayra. Sixteen of the forty-nine requested links were rejected as not downloadable, Editorial-licensed, or otherwise unlicensed (Porsche GT3RS, Porsche Panamera, Porsche Singer 911 Turbo, Rolls-Royce Mansory Phantom, Rolls-Royce Silver Cloud, two separate Rolls-Royce Cullinan candidates, Rolls-Royce BoatTail, Mercedes-Benz C-Class 2022, BMW M4 Coupe, a further Kia Forte candidate, Pagani Utopia 2023, and three Land Rover Defender candidates). Two requested links were exact Sketchfab-UID duplicates of sources already in the collection (a Mercedes-Benz 300 SL Gullwing matching the existing Mercedes 300 SL, and a "[FREE] BMW M3 E30" matching the existing BMW M3 E30) and were not re-added. Three further requested links were real-world duplicates of cars already in the collection under different sources — a Porsche 959, a Ferrari LaFerrari, and a Ferrari 250 GTO — and were skipped as redundant; a requested Mercedes-Benz 190/W201 pair resolved to the same real car under two different sources, so only one (Dave Love SketchFab) was kept. Two sources in this batch needed the `x-length` orientation flag instead of `native`: the Mercedes-Maybach and BMW M4 CSL sources both use their raw file's X axis as vehicle length rather than the pipeline's default Y, and converting either with `native` inflated the reported width to over 11 meters by scaling the true (short) Y-range onto the target length. The Mercedes-Maybach source additionally included a stray default-primitive reference cube (exact ±1-unit vertices, no relation to the vehicle) positioned below the car, skewing its floor and height calculation the same way the Ford GT40's stray spheres once did; excluding it by name fixed the model's floor placement. The Kia Forte was replaced for a second time: its previous source (an AI 3D-reconstruction with foggy glass, a garbled dashboard-mounted sticker, and no separate glass or cabin geometry) was swapped for a hand-modeled 2010 Kia Forte Koup (Ddiaz Design, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) with clean glass, a modeled interior, and a correctly badged grille.

A sixth pass replaced the Ferrari Roma and added a Lamborghini Aventador SVJ, a Tesla Roadster, and fourteen more Formula 1 cars spanning 2019–2023. The Ferrari Roma's original source (zizian) was replaced with a cleaner one (Ddiaz Design, CC BY 4.0) at the user's request; a second requested replacement link for the same car turned out not to be downloadable. Of a further batch of requested links, several were rejected outright: a 1965 Ferrari 330 GT and an Aventador SVJ carried a Sketchfab "Free Standard" license (not a Creative Commons license, despite resembling one at a glance); an Audi Q7 "demo" model, a Tesla Model 3, and a Tesla Cybertruck were all not downloadable (the Cybertruck despite carrying a CC BY-NC-SA tag); and a repeated F1 Mercedes W11 2020 link was confirmed Editorial-licensed again, consistent with an earlier rejection of the same link. A Lamborghini SVJ Carbonado Twin Turbo Mansory (VTX) was downloaded and converted, but its source had a large flat floor/backdrop object whose exclusion, combined with the pipeline's length-normalization step, left the model roughly 5 meters wide regardless of the `native` or `x-length` orientation flag tried; after three fix attempts it was dropped rather than shipped broken, and the collection's one Aventador SVJ instead uses a cleaner stock-styled source (Ddiaz Design, CC BY 4.0). A Dodge Challenger (Gesy, CC BY 4.0) was downloaded and attempted twice; both conversion attempts hung indefinitely (each run for 25+ minutes with active but unproductive CPU use) on the same source file and were killed, the same failure mode seen earlier with the Mustang "Eleanor" source, so it was not added. Of twenty-two requested F1 links spanning 2019–2024, sixteen were accepted after license/downloadability checks; five candidates from one account (2024-season McLaren MCL-38 in two forms, Ferrari SF-24, Haas VF-24, and Sauber C-44) all carried a Sketchfab "Standard" license and were rejected. A duplicate Alpine A521 was requested from two different sources; the higher-detail one (Metazeon, 750k faces) had two objects sharing a material spanning a huge, clearly non-vehicle area whose exclusion — like the Carbonado above — only shifted the bug to the next-widest stray geometry rather than fixing it, so the collection uses the lower-detail but clean OUTPISTON source instead, matching the rest of that creator's consistent 2019–2021 F1 series (nine cars in this batch: the W10, C41, FW42, C38, RS19, RP20, AT01, SF21, FW43, A521, and RS20 are all OUTPISTON, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). A McLaren MP4/27 (Maxence Leret d'Aubigny, CC BY 4.0) had a large ground-shadow plane excluded successfully, but the underlying source turned out to have multiple wing and bodywork pieces mirrored or duplicated several meters off to one side — a systemic geometry problem rather than one stray prop — so it was dropped rather than shipped with visibly broken wings, the same judgment call made for the original Mercedes-AMG GT source. The Citroën 2CV was re-examined after a user report of an odd-looking rolled-back canvas roof; its materials (including the body paint) turned out to be alpha-blended for no apparent reason, the same latent-transparency class of bug fixed earlier for the Lotus 72 and Ferrari F2004, so they are now forced opaque during conversion (excluding the windshield and glass, which are meant to be transparent) — the roof's rolled-back-canvas appearance itself did not change, since visual inspection confirmed it matches the real Citroën 2CV Charleston's folding fabric sunroof design rather than being a rendering defect.
